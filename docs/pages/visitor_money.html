Niagara Falls simulation · 2025 · 60 scenarios that fit the evidence

Where visitor money goes

Visitors spend about a year at Niagara Falls businesses. A quarter of the businesses, packed around the Falls, take almost three quarters of it. The rest of the city shares what's left, and that is where the closed storefronts are.

Who receives it

The province owns the two casinos and Niagara Parks. Chains and brands include hotel brands and franchises, whose fees and profits mostly leave the city. Independents are everyone else, including large local owners of hotels and Clifton Hill attractions.

They traded in 2025 in the simulation; forward outlooks leave them out. Across all scenarios, closed businesses earned less in 2025 than open neighbours of the same kind 55% of the time (50% would be no signal).

The evidence every scenario had to match

800 combinations of the model's unknown settings were run. 172 matched every money fact; the 60 tested further also matched road traffic counts and monthly hotel occupancy. Government figures are labelled and allowed a wider margin, as the project's rules require.

What the data settles, and what it doesn't

  • Settled: visitor numbers and spending are within about 10% of the official figures; a business's size has to matter strongly.
  • Open: how far visitors wander from the Falls. This alone moves the Falls area's share between 68% and 75%. Street counts on Lundy's Lane and Clifton Hill, or Google review counts by location, would narrow it.
  • Open: how much of visitors' restaurant money stays in the city. It sets how concentrated the top 10 are.
  • Not in the model yet: where money goes after the till. Franchise fees, owners' profits taken elsewhere, sales tax and commuters' wages all leave the city.
